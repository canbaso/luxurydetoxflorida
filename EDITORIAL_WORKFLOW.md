# Luxury Detox Florida Editorial Research and Writing Workflow

Repository: `canbaso/luxurydetoxflorida`  
Site: `https://luxurydetoxflorida.com/`

This document is the required editorial harness for future articles. It is designed for health-related YMYL content, Florida facility comparisons, luxury detox and rehab topics, residential mental health content, sober living, costs, treatment definitions, and related SEO articles.

The goal is to produce useful, current, evidence-based articles that can be pasted into WordPress with minimal cleanup.

---

## 1. Research before drafting

Never start by writing the introduction.

Complete the research pass first.

### 1.1 Keyword research

Use Semrush before drafting a new SEO article.

For the proposed topic:

1. Check the exact phrase.
2. Check the broader root phrase.
3. Check close variants.
4. Check search intent.
5. Record:
   - U.S. search volume;
   - keyword difficulty;
   - CPC when useful for commercial intent;
   - close variants;
   - question keywords;
   - related terms.
6. Choose one primary keyword.
7. Choose secondary keywords that belong to the same intent.
8. Avoid creating two articles that answer the same search intent.

Do not force the exact keyword into the title when a broader phrase has materially stronger demand and the same intent.

Example:

If `luxury sober living florida` has lower search volume than `sober living florida` and `luxury sober living`, the H1 can target the exact Florida intent while the article naturally covers the larger terms.

### 1.2 SERP research

Search the live web for:

- the exact target keyword;
- close variants;
- "best" and "top" versions when the article is a ranking;
- cost queries when price is part of the intent;
- definitions when the topic is ambiguous.

Identify what Google is currently rewarding:

- list article;
- treatment center page;
- directory;
- informational guide;
- comparison;
- local landing page;
- government resource.

Do not copy the SERP. Use it to understand intent and gaps.

## 2. Facility-name discovery workflow

This step is mandatory for any article that names or ranks facilities, treatment centers, recovery residences, hospitals, sober homes, or programs.

### 2.1 Build a candidate pool first

Do not begin with ten remembered brands.

Search for facility names using multiple query patterns.

Examples:

- `luxury detox Florida`
- `medical detox Florida private room`
- `residential mental health Florida [condition]`
- `ADHD residential treatment Florida`
- `luxury sober living Florida`
- `FARR certified luxury sober living`
- `site:myflfamilies.com recovery residence [name]`
- `site:ahca.myflorida.com residential treatment [name]`
- `[facility name] [specific service]`
- `[facility name] residential mental health`
- `[facility name] medical detox`
- `[facility name] FARR`
- `[facility name] private room`
- `[facility name] Florida address`

When the market allows, compile at least 15 candidates for a Top 10 article.

The candidate pool can come from:

- Google/web search;
- official facility websites;
- Florida DCF;
- Florida AHCA;
- FARR;
- SAMHSA FindTreatment;
- Joint Commission;
- CARF;
- ASAM certification resources;
- reputable treatment directories for discovery only.

Third-party directories may help discover names. They should not be the sole source for important facility claims.

### 2.2 Verify every candidate

Open the official facility website.

Confirm:

- exact facility name;
- current Florida location;
- whether the location is actually operating;
- the exact level of care;
- population served;
- gender restrictions;
- age restrictions;
- service named in the article;
- whether detox is onsite or referred elsewhere;
- whether "residential" means true residential treatment or outpatient care with separate housing;
- medical or psychiatric staffing when claimed;
- private-room availability when claimed;
- amenities when claimed;
- price only when currently published;
- accreditation only when currently documented;
- state license or recovery-residence certification when relevant.

Never assume that a brand's service at one location exists at all locations.

### 2.3 Verify classification

This is one of the most important checks.

Do not confuse:

- medical detox with residential rehab;
- residential rehab with sober living;
- PHP with residential treatment;
- outpatient treatment with supportive housing;
- dual-diagnosis addiction treatment with primary mental health treatment;
- a "mental health retreat" with licensed residential psychiatric care;
- recovery coaching with clinical treatment.

For sober living:

- treat it as recovery housing unless the organization separately operates licensed treatment;
- verify FARR status for the exact residence when possible;
- do not describe sober living as detox, rehab, psychiatric hospitalization, or inpatient care.

For mental health:

- verify that the facility treats the mental health condition named in the article;
- do not infer ADHD, OCD, schizophrenia, bipolar, trauma, or other condition-specific care from a generic mental-health page.

### 2.4 Remove weak candidates

Exclude a candidate when:

- the facility is closed;
- the Florida location cannot be verified;
- the named service is not actually offered;
- the service is only outpatient when the article requires residential care;
- "luxury" is unsupported by privacy, accommodation, service level, or environment;
- the facility is primarily an addiction program when the article requires primary mental-health treatment;
- the official website is too vague to verify core claims;
- current licensing or certification conflicts with the marketing claim.

Do not pad a Top 10 list. A smaller accurate list is better than ten questionable entries.

## 3. Facility image research workflow

Facility images are part of the research process, not a final cosmetic pass.

For every facility that survives verification and is likely to appear in the final ranking:

### 3.1 Search for the actual facility

Search using combinations such as:

- `[facility name] [city] exterior`
- `[facility name] campus`
- `[facility name] residence`
- `[facility name] accommodations`
- `[facility name] official photos`
- `site:[official domain] [facility name] photos`

Use image search in addition to normal web search when it helps locate the facility's current property images.

### 3.2 Image-source priority

Prefer sources in this order:

1. official facility website;
2. official property/program page;
3. official press or media kit;
4. official organization social account when reuse is permitted;
5. clearly licensed third-party image source.

Do not use:

- unrelated stock photography;
- another facility's building;
- Google review photos without a clear right to republish;
- screenshots of Google Maps;
- images from directories merely because they rank in search;
- AI-generated images presented as the real facility;
- watermarked images;
- outdated images when a current facility image is available.

### 3.3 Verify the image

Before including an image, confirm:

- it depicts the named facility, campus, residence, or a clearly identified space within it;
- it belongs to the correct Florida location;
- it is current enough to represent the property;
- the source page is preserved;
- the direct image URL or media asset is preserved when available;
- any required attribution or license language is recorded.

If the facility has multiple Florida properties, identify which property the image shows. Do not use a photo from one location to illustrate another without saying so.

### 3.4 Article placement

For ranked articles, aim for one facility image per ranked entry.

Place the image after the facility H3 and "Best for" line, before or near the main descriptive paragraphs.

Preferred WordPress pattern:

```html
<figure style="margin:20px 0;">
  <img src="VERIFIED-IMAGE-URL" alt="Facility Name in City, Florida" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;">
  <figcaption>Facility Name, City, Florida. Image: Source/Facility Name.</figcaption>
</figure>
```

Use a caption only when attribution, clarification, or property identification is useful.

Do not invent the image URL. If a reusable direct image URL is not available, preserve the source page and flag the image for upload rather than fabricating markup.

### 3.5 Image SEO

Alt text must:

- identify the facility;
- identify the Florida city when useful;
- describe what is visibly shown;
- stay factual;
- avoid keyword stuffing.

Good:

> RECO Institute recovery residence with swimming pool in Delray Beach, Florida

Bad:

> best luxury detox rehab Florida top treatment center addiction recovery

For an image showing a bedroom, pool, exterior, waterfront property, dining area, or campus, say so accurately.

### 3.6 Image evidence record

During research, maintain an image record for each ranked facility containing:

- facility name;
- property/location;
- source page URL;
- direct image URL when available;
- what the image depicts;
- attribution/license note when applicable;
- proposed alt text.

The article should not claim that an image depicts a facility unless this evidence exists.

## 4. Ranking rubric

Rank facilities only after verification.

The exact weighting can change by article, but use the following framework.

### 3.1 Clinical or service fit: highest weight

Ask:

- Does the facility actually provide the service named in the title?
- Is the level of care appropriate?
- Is medical or psychiatric capability documented?
- Is there meaningful treatment structure?
- Does the facility handle the population named in the article?

### 3.2 Verification and regulatory strength

Give weight to:

- Florida license or current regulatory listing;
- FARR certification for recovery residences;
- Joint Commission accreditation;
- CARF accreditation;
- ASAM certification where relevant;
- clear official documentation.

Accreditation is not proof of superior outcomes. Do not write as if it is.

### 3.3 Privacy and luxury features

Consider:

- private bedrooms;
- private bathrooms;
- small census;
- secluded or gated property;
- waterfront or estate setting;
- executive support;
- chef-prepared meals;
- fitness facilities;
- pools;
- wellness services;
- transportation;
- higher staff attention;
- individual therapy frequency where verified.

Luxury features should never outrank the required clinical or recovery function.

### 3.4 Continuity of care

For treatment facilities, consider:

- detox to residential transition;
- residential to PHP/IOP transition;
- psychiatric follow-up;
- medication management;
- aftercare planning.

For sober living, consider:

- recovery accountability;
- testing;
- house management;
- employment or school expectations;
- meeting access;
- transportation;
- connection with outpatient care.

### 3.5 Editorial ranking language

Use:

- "Our strongest overall choice..."
- "Best for..."
- "Worth comparing when..."
- "Ranks highly because..."

Do not claim:

- "highest success rate";
- "best outcomes";
- "most effective";
- "number one in Florida";

unless reliable comparative outcome data actually supports the claim.

## 5. Medical and legal research standards

This site covers YMYL topics. Medical, legal, insurance, and safety statements require stronger sources than ordinary lifestyle copy.

### Preferred clinical sources

Use current information from:

- NIDA;
- NIAAA;
- SAMHSA;
- CDC;
- NIH;
- NIMH;
- FDA;
- DEA;
- ASAM;
- APA;
- peer-reviewed journals and PubMed.

### Florida regulatory sources

Use:

- Florida Department of Children and Families;
- Florida Agency for Health Care Administration;
- Florida Legislature and current Florida Statutes;
- FARR for recovery-residence certification;
- official state licensing databases.

### Facility claims

Primary source should normally be the facility's current official website, then cross-check important regulatory claims.

### Avoid

Do not use another treatment center's blog as the authority for a general medical claim when NIDA, NIAAA, SAMHSA, CDC, ASAM, or a peer-reviewed source is available.

Do not use a competitor's marketing article as evidence that another competitor provides a service.

## 6. Writing Iteration: prose harness

### 5.1 Direct statement over performance

Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement.

Instead of:

> a parameter worth varying

write:

> a factor that may change the result

Instead of:

> this point earns its keep

write:

> this point still matters

The prose should serve the reader, not display the writer.

### 5.2 Prohibited style habits

Do not use:

- decorative metaphors where a literal sentence works;
- "a beacon of hope";
- "a journey toward healing";
- "sits at the intersection of";
- "a tapestry of";
- "a testament to";
- "a dial worth turning";
- "earns its keep";
- "in today's fast-paced world";
- "when it comes to";
- "whether you are X or Y";
- repetitive "This isn't X. It's Y." constructions;
- generic emotional claims;
- empty reassurance;
- fake patient stories;
- fabricated expert quotations.

Do not use em dashes.

Avoid:

- perhaps;
- imagine;
- embrace;
- vital;
- excellence;
- perilous;
- chill pill.

### 5.3 Sentence and paragraph style

Use:

- direct sentences;
- concrete nouns;
- exact treatment names;
- exact facility names;
- exact locations;
- short to medium paragraphs;
- enough variation that the article does not become telegram-style prose.

Prefer:

> Luxury rehab usually begins after medical stabilization.

over:

> Once the storm of withdrawal has passed, the next chapter of the healing journey can begin.

Prefer:

> The facility offers private rooms and a six-client maximum.

over:

> Privacy takes center stage in an intimate setting built around discretion.

### 5.4 Avoid unnecessary hedging

Use uncertainty only when the evidence is uncertain.

Good:

> The facility states that private rooms are available.

Good:

> Published rates were not available when this article was reviewed.

Bad:

> The facility perhaps offers what may be considered a potentially private experience.

### 5.5 Consumer-facing copy

Do not expose internal SEO logic inside the article.

Do not write:

- search volume;
- keyword difficulty;
- "we included this because Google...";
- crawler or indexing notes;
- internal research exclusions;
- tool failures;
- data-pipeline commentary.

The research process can be rigorous without becoming part of the consumer-facing copy.

## 7. Article structure

Default target: 2,500 to 3,000 words unless a different length is requested.

### 6.1 SEO metadata before the article

Provide:

- SEO title;
- focus keyword;
- secondary keywords;
- suggested slug;
- meta description.

### 6.2 WordPress article structure

Default output:

```html
<section>
<h1>...</h1>
<p>...</p>
</section>
```

Use one H1.

Use H2s for major sections and H3s for facility entries and FAQ questions.

Avoid unnecessary `<div>` elements.

### 6.3 Opening

Answer the search intent immediately.

Within the first 150 words:

- define the subject;
- state the primary distinction;
- explain who the page is for.

Do not begin with generic context about how addiction or mental illness affects millions of Americans unless that statistic is necessary.

### 6.4 Methodology section for ranked lists

Explain the actual inclusion rules in plain language.

Examples:

- true onsite detox;
- true residential mental-health care;
- explicit ADHD treatment;
- certified recovery residence;
- private or luxury characteristics;
- psychiatric services;
- current Florida location.

Do not write a fake scoring system unless one was actually used.

### 6.5 Comparison table

For Top 10 articles, include a table before long facility profiles.

Useful columns:

- Facility;
- Location;
- Best For;
- Standout Features.

Use inline styles when the article must paste cleanly into WordPress.

Do not add unsupported star ratings or invented scores.

### 6.6 Facility profiles

Each ranked facility should normally include:

- rank and facility name;
- location;
- "Best for" statement;
- why it qualified;
- exact service scope;
- meaningful clinical or recovery details;
- luxury/privacy details;
- important limitation where relevant;
- official source link.

Avoid repeating the same paragraph structure ten times.

Do not turn each profile into promotional copy.

### 6.7 Educational sections after the ranking

Add only sections that answer real follow-up questions.

Depending on topic:

- what luxury means;
- detox vs rehab;
- sober living vs rehab;
- who needs residential care;
- cost;
- insurance;
- length of stay;
- private rooms;
- medication;
- licensing/certification;
- how to choose;
- questions to ask.

### 6.8 Internal links

Link only to relevant existing pages.

Use contextual anchor text.

Examples for this content cluster may include:

- `/what-does-luxury-detox-mean/`
- `/luxury-detox-centers-florida/`
- `/luxury-alcohol-detox-cost/`
- `/what-is-luxury-rehab/`
- `/luxury-detox-vs-luxury-rehab/`
- `/luxury-mental-health-facilities-florida/`
- `/residential-adhd-treatment-florida/`
- `/luxury-sober-living-florida/`

Verify that a destination exists before publishing the link.

Do not overlink the same page repeatedly.

## 8. Cost articles

Never invent an "average" when no reliable average exists.

Separate:

- published facility rates;
- published market estimates;
- standard treatment estimates;
- luxury examples;
- detox-only pricing;
- 30-day residential pricing.

Explain when two prices are not directly comparable.

For example:

A 7-day detox quote should not be compared as if it were equivalent to a 30-day residential-rehab package.

Use current published rates only. Include the date context when rates are likely to change.

---

## 9. Sober-living articles

Keep sober living distinct from treatment.

Required checks:

- recovery residence vs treatment facility;
- FARR certification when claimed;
- exact property, not only brand-level certification;
- gender served;
- occupancy if claimed;
- private-room availability;
- house rules;
- testing;
- curfew;
- meeting expectations;
- employment/school expectations;
- medication policies;
- pricing only when published.

Never write that a sober home provides medical detox unless it separately operates and clearly documents licensed detox services.

For Florida law and certification, verify the current statute and DCF/FARR information at the time of writing.

---

## 10. Mental-health articles

Do not assume that an addiction facility with a dual-diagnosis page is a primary mental-health residential facility.

For a condition-specific article, verify the condition by name.

Examples:

- ADHD;
- OCD;
- bipolar disorder;
- schizophrenia;
- PTSD;
- depression;
- anxiety.

If the condition is not explicitly within current program scope, do not rank the facility based on inference.

For ADHD specifically, do not imply that residential treatment is routine first-line care. Explain that outpatient medication, psychotherapy, and behavioral treatment are common, and that residential care is more relevant when impairment or co-occurring conditions justify a higher level of care.

---

## 11. Detox and rehab articles

Detox and rehab are different stages of care.

### Detox

Focus on:

- withdrawal management;
- medical monitoring;
- stabilization;
- medication when appropriate;
- immediate safety;
- transition to further treatment.

### Rehab

Focus on:

- residential addiction treatment;
- therapy;
- psychiatric care;
- medication management;
- relapse prevention;
- family work;
- discharge planning.

Do not describe a rehab as having onsite detox unless the facility currently documents onsite withdrawal-management capability.

## 12. FAQs

Use FAQs to answer actual decision questions.

Good FAQ subjects:

- cost;
- insurance;
- length of stay;
- private rooms;
- medication;
- who qualifies;
- detox requirement;
- residential vs inpatient;
- licensing or certification;
- how to verify a facility.

Avoid FAQs that merely repeat H2 sections word for word without adding a concise answer.

When FAQ content is included in the article, add matching `FAQPage` JSON-LD.

The schema wording must match the visible FAQ content closely.

---

## 13. Structured data

### Ranked lists

Use `ItemList` with:

- correct item count;
- correct ranking order;
- exact facility names.

Do not add fabricated ratings, reviews, addresses, or medical specialties to schema.

### FAQs

Use `FAQPage` only when the questions and answers are visible on the page.

### Article schema

Do not add duplicate Article schema when the WordPress SEO plugin already generates it unless specifically requested.

---

## 14. Sources section

Ranked articles should include a Sources section.

Source order:

1. government or regulator;
2. authoritative medical organization;
3. peer-reviewed research;
4. official facility pages.

For facility lists, link to the exact program/property page where possible rather than only the homepage.

Do not cite a source that does not actually support the statement.

## 15. Final quality-control pass

Before delivery, check all of the following.

### Research

- [ ] Semrush research completed.
- [ ] Search intent confirmed.
- [ ] Live SERP checked.
- [ ] Facility names discovered through current search rather than memory alone.
- [ ] Candidate pool larger than final ranking when possible.
- [ ] Every ranked facility checked on its official website.
- [ ] Current Florida location verified.
- [ ] Exact service verified.
- [ ] Licensing/certification claims cross-checked when relevant.
- [ ] Prices are current and published, not inferred.
- [ ] A current image was searched for every ranked facility.
- [ ] Each used image was verified as the correct facility/property.
- [ ] Image source pages and direct image URLs were recorded where available.
- [ ] Reuse/attribution requirements were checked before publication.

### Accuracy

- [ ] Detox is not confused with rehab.
- [ ] Rehab is not confused with sober living.
- [ ] Outpatient care is not described as residential.
- [ ] Dual diagnosis is not automatically described as primary mental-health treatment.
- [ ] No outcome claims without comparative evidence.
- [ ] No invented bed counts, prices, amenities, staffing, accreditation, or insurance claims.

### Prose

- [ ] No em dashes.
- [ ] No mannered prose.
- [ ] No decorative metaphor.
- [ ] No "AI slop" opening.
- [ ] No generic inspirational language.
- [ ] No search-volume discussion inside article copy.
- [ ] No repetitive "this isn't X, it's Y" construction.
- [ ] Paragraphs are readable and not telegram-style.
- [ ] Claims are specific and direct.

### WordPress

- [ ] One H1.
- [ ] Section-based HTML.
- [ ] Comparison table included when useful.
- [ ] Relevant internal links.
- [ ] Sources section.
- [ ] FAQ section.
- [ ] FAQ schema matches visible FAQ.
- [ ] ItemList schema matches ranking.
- [ ] No unnecessary divs, icons, canvas, or emojis.
- [ ] Each ranked facility has a verified image when a usable image exists.
- [ ] Facility images are placed with the matching profile.
- [ ] Alt text is factual and descriptive, not keyword-stuffed.
- [ ] No generic stock image is presented as a real facility.

## 16. Delivery rule

For a full article, deliver:

1. SEO title;
2. focus keyword;
3. secondary keywords;
4. slug;
5. meta description;
6. complete WordPress-ready article;
7. table;
8. FAQs;
9. sources;
10. schema;
11. verified facility images embedded with the matching profiles when usable images are available;
12. image source/attribution information needed for publication.

Do not stop after research unless the user explicitly asks for research only.

When the user says "next article," apply this workflow automatically before drafting.
